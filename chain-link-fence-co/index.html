How a job goes

Four steps. One crew. No surprises.

Walk the line

Free visit. We measure, check grade and utilities, confirm the property line with you, and hand you a written number.

Set the posts

Tennessee 811 called. Holes dug below the frost line, posts plumbed and set in concrete, then left to cure.

Rail and stretch

Top rail and tension wire on, mesh or boards run tight and level, cut ends capped.

Hang and walk through

Gates hung and adjusted, site cleaned, old fence hauled off, and a walk-through with you before we leave.

Estimate

Questions

The ones we get first.

How much does a fence cost?

Type, height, footage, gates, and the ground. Chain link is the least per foot, vinyl the most. The estimate is free and in writing, so you have a real number before anything gets dug.

Do I need a permit?

Inside Dickson city limits, sometimes. In the county, usually not for a residential fence. We'll tell you what applies and handle the paperwork if there is any.

What about my property line?

We build to the line you show us and flag anything that looks off. If you're not sure, a survey before we dig is cheaper than moving a fence after.

Will you call before you dig?

Yes. Tennessee 811 before every job so gas, water, and power get marked.

How long does it take?

Most residential jobs are one to three days once materials are on site. Posts first, concrete cures, then the fence goes up.

Can you fix my existing fence instead of replacing it?

Often. Leaning posts, bent rails, sagging gates, and storm damage are everyday repairs. If the posts are rotted through, replacing is the honest answer, and we'll say so.
